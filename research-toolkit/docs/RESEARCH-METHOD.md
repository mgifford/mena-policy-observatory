# Comparative legal research methodology

**Unit of analysis:** one discrete provision and one narrowly defined policy question, not one country score.

Research dimensions per provision:
1. Exact source / issuer / document type / authoritative language / URL / captured bytes / checksum / locator.
2. Jurisdiction (country, territory, subnational), sector, covered persons and entities.
3. Normative type: right, duty, prohibition, permission, institutional_power, safeguard, definition, standard_reference, guidance.
4. Modal language: must, shall, may, should, prohibited, discretionary; preserve original language.
5. Trigger, conditions, exceptions, dates, transitional clauses, amendments, supersession and relationship sources.
6. Enforcement and remedies, with separate evidence of actual implementation.
7. Competing interpretation, unknown scope and reviewer dissent.
8. Formalizability: deterministic / conditional / human_judgment / unsuitable / not_assessed.

## Source search protocol
Start with official gazettes, parliamentary and ministry sites, regulators, judiciary, treaty databases, and authoritative standards bodies. Search in applicable languages: Arabic, French, English, Persian, Hebrew, Somali and other relevant national languages. Maintain a search log (queries, sites, dates, findings and gaps). Consult secondary inventories for discovery; verify legal assertions from primary instruments.

## Negative findings
'No law identified' is only allowed with a published search scope, language list, repositories searched, date and limitations. Default to 'research incomplete'. Never infer no law from an empty registry.

## Temporal effects
Record source-published, promulgation, entry-into-force, amendment and repeal separately. An effective date before publication can be lawful; flag for review, do not automatically discard. For a scenario date, only evaluate verified applicable versions; ambiguous transitions yield `indeterminate`.

## Sample / coverage caveat
Do not equate the number of policy records with a jurisdiction's policy quality or a country's rights protections. Never use comparative rankings based on incomplete evidence. Disclose selection criteria and missing-source patterns.
